EARLY INTERVENTION · THE FIRST 2,000 DAYS

The years that shape everything

The first 2,000 days - from birth to around five and a half years - are when a child’s brain develops most rapidly. Starting support during this window helps build stronger foundations for communication, learning, social connection and everyday life.

Every child’s journey is different. But when support begins early and stays consistent, each stage builds on the one before it. Here’s what that journey can look like.

Age 2

Identifying the early signs

For many families, this is when questions begin. You may notice differences in speech, play, communication or the way your child connects with others. Recognising these early signs is not about having all the answers - it’s about knowing when to take the next step.

Age 3–4

Building a strong foundation

With the right support in place, these early years become an opportunity to strengthen communication, social interaction, learning and everyday routines. Small, consistent steps begin to build lasting skills.

Age 5

Preparing for new experiences

As children begin school, the skills they’ve been developing at home and in therapy can help them navigate new routines, relationships and learning environments with greater confidence.

Age 6–7

Supporting long-term growth

Development doesn’t stop at one milestone. As children grow, ongoing support, shared goals and meaningful progress tracking help families celebrate how far they’ve come and plan for what’s next.

Every milestone is a moment worth celebrating
